# Human Writing Pro

Create writing that earns the reader's attention through useful substance, precise language, and a recognizable voice. Naturalness is an editorial judgment, not a detector score.

## Establish the brief

Infer the deliverable, audience, purpose, channel, language, length, and voice from the request and available context. Ask only when a missing detail would materially change the result; otherwise make a reasonable choice and draft. Do not make users complete an intake questionnaire for a simple title or rewrite.

Use supplied brand guidance and examples first. With voice samples, identify observable traits: formality, point of view, vocabulary, rhythm, humor, emotional intensity, and paragraph shape. Preserve distinctive choices that serve the reader. A short sample supports tentative style choices, not confident claims about the writer's personality. Do not infer the user's language or cultural preferences from their name or location.

When close voice matching matters, prefer samples from the same genre and audience. Keep a short working profile of observable choices, compare the draft against those samples, and incorporate the user's corrections. Treat matching as an approximation, not proof of authorship. Preserve meaningful dialect, cultural references, and idiosyncratic phrasing unless the user requests standardization or they obstruct the intended reader. Never make every author sound like the default brand voice.

Without samples, default to clear, warm, professionally conversational prose. Adjust tone to the reader's situation: calm for problems, direct for decisions, lively when appropriate. Do not impose casual language on formal work.

## Choose the work mode

- New content: identify the reader's actual question and a useful angle; organize around the answer, explanation, evidence, and next step as appropriate.
- Revision: preserve meaning, factual qualifications, commitments, numbers, names, links, and the author's stance. Match edit depth to the request. Do not rewrite good passages just to make them different.
- Titles and descriptions: read format guidance (included below under Reference: formats.md) for promise, specificity, alternatives, and length handling.
- Articles, marketing copy, or social content: read the relevant section of format guidance (included below under Reference: formats.md).
- For editorial calibration, consult examples and review cases (included below under Reference: editorial-review.md). Consult research notes (included below under Reference: research.md) when explaining the design or updating its guidance.
- When the user requests AI-detector checks or asks how detection works, read detector evidence and testing (included below under Reference: detector-checks.md). Keep observed tool results separate from writing quality and known authorship.

## Develop substance before polishing

Make each section answer a real reader question. Choose details that explain how something works, when it matters, what the tradeoffs are, or what the reader can do. Do not pad a requested word count by restating the same point.

Separate supplied facts, verified facts, and inference. Research current, disputed, specialist, or consequential claims when needed. Prefer original sources and cite supporting pages near claims in research content. Never manufacture statistics, quotations, testimonials, results, product features, credentials, or personal experience. Clearly signal hypothetical examples. If browsing is unavailable, narrow or qualify the claim and disclose a material verification gap.

First-person storytelling requires facts supplied by the user or an explicitly fictional brief. Do not invent an anecdote to make factual writing feel human. Preserve uncertainty where evidence is uncertain. In editing, flag a questionable claim instead of silently strengthening it.

## Write and edit

Start where the reader's interest begins: a useful answer, concrete problem, relevant observation, or supported scene. Skip generic historical introductions and announcements about what the article will discuss unless orientation is genuinely needed.

Use specific nouns and verbs. Replace abstract praise with a supported detail or remove it. Keep necessary terminology and explain it at the reader's level. Prefer familiar words when they preserve meaning; do not swap precise terms for loose synonyms merely to create variety.

Reduce avoidable reading effort: unpack dense noun chains, keep a subject near its verb, clarify ambiguous pronouns, and explain a causal step the reader would otherwise have to guess. Preserve technical precision and meaningful complexity. Do not maximize vocabulary diversity, sentence-length variation, or a readability score as a proxy for quality; no single numerical target defines human writing.

Let sentence length follow the thought. Use short sentences for clear emphasis and longer ones for connected reasoning. Avoid mechanical alternation, forced fragments, repetitive paragraph sizes, and a rhetorical question at the start of every section. Contractions, humor, asides, and punctuation are choices governed by voice, not quotas.

Remove unnecessary framing, generic transitions, repeated conclusions, overstatement, and empty urgency. Examine stock phrases such as 'in today's fast-paced world', 'game-changing', and 'unlock your potential' in context; improve the underlying thought rather than applying a blind banned-word list. Do not universally forbid passive voice, dashes, three-item lists, or contrasts when they carry meaning.

Review patterns across the whole article: repeated sentence openings, interchangeable section templates, generic hypothetical scenes, several sentences restating one idea, and advice too vague to act on. Keep a pattern when it serves the piece; otherwise rewrite the affected passage around a specific, supported point. For example, 'You still own them. They are still available.' may repeat the meaning already conveyed by saying items can be stored nearby. Replace filler with a relevant constraint, example, or explanation only when it is known or clearly hypothetical. These are editorial checks, not established detector rules.

Keep paragraph connections logical. Use headings for navigation, lists for genuinely parallel information, and tables for comparisons. Avoid formatting every sentence as a separate dramatic paragraph. End when the reader has the needed answer; add a conclusion or call to action only when it helps.

For persuasive writing, connect an actual feature to a plausible reader benefit and support stronger claims with evidence. Never invent scarcity, guarantees, social proof, or urgency.

## Final editorial pass

Check the whole piece, not just individual phrases:

1. Purpose: Does it deliver the promise of the title and answer the intended reader's question?
2. Fidelity: Are facts, conditions, qualifications, and the author's stance intact? Are new factual claims supported?
3. Voice: Does the register fit the brief? Are forced friendliness, fake anecdotes, or generic brand praise absent?
4. Flow: Does each paragraph contribute? Remove repetition and abrupt logical jumps.
5. Constraints: Verify requested length, number of options, required terms, and formatting. Count characters when a hard limit matters; do not estimate and label it exact.

For informative articles and web copy, also check findability and comprehension: can the intended reader locate the main answer from the opening and headings, explain the main point, and identify the next action when one is needed? Treat your own review as an editorial check, not observed user research. If asked to verify effectiveness, distinguish instruction coverage, sample-based checks, and actual reader testing. For high-value recurring content, recommend blind comparisons judged by intended readers on usefulness, clarity, trust, and voice fit. Do not run reader studies or publish content without authorization.

Revise material failures before delivery. Do not produce invented 'human scores', authorship probabilities, or promises to evade AI detectors. Do not insert errors to simulate a person.

Return the finished content in the requested format. Keep research notes and editorial explanations separate, and include them only when useful or requested. Do not expose internal drafts or checklists by default. Creating copy does not authorize publishing or sending it.

# Reference: detector-checks.md

# Detector evidence and testing

Reviewed 10 November 2026. Apply this reference only when detection is part of the request. A skill changes instructions, not the underlying model or a detector's proprietary classifier. Never guarantee universal human classification or misstate generated text as human-authored.

## What the evidence establishes

Statistical detectors may examine how probable words or sequences are under a language model. Other detectors learn distinctions from examples of human, generated, edited, and mixed text. Linguistic repetition, structure, vocabulary, and contextual relationships may contribute to learned distinctions; proprietary feature weights and thresholds are generally not available. A list of stock phrases is not a complete detector specification.

GPTZero's support page says it stopped using perplexity and burstiness in autumn 2023. Its 2026 technical report describes supervised deep learning with document and sentence predictions, hierarchical classes, and training on rewritten text. Exact architecture and hyperparameters are proprietary. This is vendor documentation, not independent proof of its advertised accuracy.
Sources:
- https://support.gptzero.me/articles/9585228410-how-do-i-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity
- https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13042v1

GPTZero also acknowledges both false positives and false negatives, and says short passages are less reliable than whole documents. Do not infer how a title or description would score from an article's score.
Source: https://support.gptzero.me/articles/1787085022-what-are-the-limitations-of-gptzeros-ai-classifier

Turnitin says its percentage describes qualifying prose flagged by the model; it is not a probability that the author used AI. Its guidance warns of misclassification, suppresses exact results below 20%, and requires at least 300 words of qualifying long-form prose. Verify current requirements before use.
Source: https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report

RAID (ACL 2024) evaluated twelve detectors against a benchmark spanning multiple models and domains and found sensitivity to changes in generation and text. These historical findings establish generalization concerns, not current performance numbers for every service.
Source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.674/

## Apply findings to writing

Improve unnecessary repetition, vague content, mechanical section structure, and mismatched register through the main editorial workflow. Use authentic user-supplied details when relevant. Preserve good prose, meaningful repetition, factual qualifications, and the user's voice. Do not impose a numerical randomness or sentence-variation target. Do not add mistakes, fabricated experiences, invisible characters, or misleading provenance claims to change a score.

A detector highlight can identify a passage to inspect, but does not prove a specific phrase caused the result. Revise when there is an identifiable writing problem and check meaning afterward. Passing a detector is not evidence of factual accuracy, originality, or actual human authorship.

## Reproducible checks when requested

1. Freeze the exact submitted text, including whether the title, description, and headings are present. Preserve a plain-text copy and a content hash when practical.
2. Use the requested service or a small explicitly identified set of accessible services. Authorization to check the supplied text permits submission of that text; do not submit unrelated private material. Do not purchase access or create accounts without authorization.
3. Record date, service URL, model/version if displayed, input scope, and the exact label and scores. State whether a score means class confidence or estimated text proportion; if unclear, say so.
4. Keep a screenshot or report when available. If access, minimum length, CAPTCHA, or payment blocks the check, report 'not tested' rather than guessing. Do not bypass access controls.
5. Report every completed result, including unfavorable or contradictory findings. Do not average unlike scores. A label applies only to that submitted text and service at that time.
6. If substantive revisions are made and a recheck is within the user's request, preserve both versions and report both results. Do not claim improvement without comparable observations. Stop after the agreed scope or a limited initial check; never silently run an open-ended rewrite-and-resubmit loop.

For broader verification, use multiple representative articles and human-written controls with known provenance. Separate detector behavior from intended readers' judgments of clarity, usefulness, and voice. No finite test can establish that all detectors will accept all future outputs.

# Reference: editorial-review.md

# Examples and review cases

These original examples illustrate editorial decisions, not universal templates.

## Concrete product copy

Facts supplied: a notebook has 160 numbered pages and lies flat.

Weak: 'Unlock boundless creativity with a revolutionary notebook designed to elevate every idea.'

Better: 'Keep notes and sketches across 160 numbered pages. The notebook lies flat while you write.'

Reason: Uses supplied details without adding paper quality, durability, or productivity claims.

## Preserve qualifications

Input: 'In our 18-person pilot, participants reported saving about 20 minutes a week. Results may differ across teams.'

Acceptable: 'Participants in our 18-person pilot said they saved about 20 minutes a week. Other teams may see different results.'

Unacceptable: 'Save 20 minutes every week, guaranteed.'

Reason: Sample, self-report, approximation, time period, and uncertainty all matter.

## Honest titles

Brief: an article explains three ways to organize a small desk.

Options: '3 Ways to Make a Small Desk Easier to Use'; 'How to Organize a Desk With Limited Space'; 'A Clearer Workspace, Even on a Small Desk'.

Reject: 'The Desk Hack That Will Double Your Productivity'. No such result was supplied.

## Review scenarios

When revising this skill, try these tasks and inspect the result against the criteria. Do not claim these are executed automated tests.

1. Rewrite the pilot statement above in a friendly voice. All factual qualifiers must survive.
2. Write a first-person product review with no usage facts supplied. Request actual experience or offer a clearly labeled hypothetical sample; do not invent ownership.
3. Write five distinct titles with a supplied hard character limit. Count the final strings; avoid unsupported promises.
4. Edit a formal notice for naturalness. Preserve obligations and conditions; do not force jokes or slang.
5. Improve a strong, concise paragraph. Make only useful changes; an unchanged paragraph can be a successful outcome.
6. Create a blog article from dated statistics. Verify current claims or label the historical scope; do not silently update numbers.

# Reference: formats.md

# Format guidance

## Articles and blog posts

Define the reader's question and one useful editorial angle. Research before making factual assertions. Choose structure to suit the subject: a practical guide may need steps; an analysis may need a claim, evidence, counterargument, and implications. Do not force every article into the same template.

Put the answer early when readers need a quick decision. Develop longer explanations with examples and relevant limitations. A narrative opening must earn its space and use verified or clearly fictional details. Prefer descriptive subheadings over clever headings that hide the topic. Include sources near substantive claims when research is part of the brief. Do not add an FAQ or SEO package unless it serves the request.

## Headlines and titles

The headline makes a promise the content must fulfill. Name the subject and useful angle. Avoid unsupported superlatives, exaggerated outcomes, and curiosity gaps that hide essential information. Use numbers only when the body actually delivers that number of items.

When asked for options, vary the angle: practical outcome, specific audience, problem, or distinctive fact. Do not return five synonyms of the same title. Recommend one briefly when selection would help. If only a title is requested, return a title rather than a whole content package.

For SEO titles, use the relevant search term naturally, distinguish the page from related pages, and consider brand naming when useful. There is no universal Google character cutoff: display truncation varies. Treat familiar character ranges as planning targets, not ranking rules. Honor a user's explicit limit and verify it.

## Descriptions

Identify the type first: product description, category copy, video description, app listing, or search meta description. These have different jobs.

Product copy: explain what it is, who it suits, the verified feature that matters, and its practical value. Include material limits or specifications when supplied. Avoid invented performance benefits.

Meta descriptions: write an accurate, distinctive preview of this page. Include a useful reason to visit without promising information absent from the page. Google may generate a different snippet; do not promise exact display or rankings. Verify hard length requirements, but do not call 160 characters a universal platform limit.

App and marketplace listings: verify current platform rules when relevant. Respect supplied positioning and field limits; do not carry website SEO conventions into every marketplace.

## Website and marketing copy

Clarify the audience, offer, supported benefit, evidence, and appropriate next action. Draft from available product facts. If key facts are missing, use modest accurate language or flag the gap outside final copy. Do not invent advantages over competitors. Make calls to action specific to what the user can actually do next.

## Social posts and newsletters

Open with a relevant point rather than manufactured drama. Keep the voice recognizable and the message focused. Adapt length and formatting to the channel. Avoid reflexive engagement bait, hashtag piles, and invented vulnerable personal stories. In newsletters, distinguish the subject line, preview text, and body when requested; make them complement each other.

## Rewriting and voice matching

Use a light edit for already effective prose. With a deeper rewrite, preserve content and reorganize only where it improves comprehension. Retain meaningful hedges such as 'may', 'up to', or 'in this sample'. Preserve quotation wording unless the task explicitly permits paraphrase, then remove quotation marks. Do not silently alter URLs, code, or field identifiers.

# Reference: research.md

# Research and design notes

Reviewed 10 November 2026. This is an original editorial skill informed by the sources below, not a copied or installed third-party package. Repository descriptions and benchmark claims are authors' reports, not independently established proof of effectiveness.

## Existing skills reviewed

### blader/humanizer
Source: https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md

The inspected skill focuses on revising AI-sounding prose. Useful features include examining repetitive rhetorical structures, preserving information during editing, and adapting to a writing sample. Its visible instructions sometimes favor aggressive pattern removal and a multi-stage output. For this broader writing task, contextual judgment and returning the finished deliverable are better defaults. Assessment is based on instruction review, not comparative testing.

### harshaneel/humanize
Source: https://github.com/harshaneel/humanize

The repository describes rewriting and AI-checking skills, voice matching, and a self-reported benchmark. Its documentation acknowledges differing detector outcomes. Voice calibration and a revision pass are useful design ideas. Detector-oriented scores and inferred authorship percentages are not adopted here. A changed model pair and self-reported sample results are insufficient grounds for general performance guarantees. This review covered repository documentation, not every implementation file.

## Editorial foundations

Mailchimp, Voice and Tone: https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/

Distinguish a stable voice from a tone adapted to the situation. Favor clarity and avoid forcing humor. Adopt that distinction without copying Mailchimp's brand personality.

Mailchimp, TL;DR: https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/tldr/

Useful content is clear and appropriate for the reader. Consistent terminology matters more than unnecessary synonym variation.

Google, Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

Focus on reader value, useful original contribution, trustworthy support, and titles that accurately describe the content. This supports research discipline and avoiding empty SEO expansion. It does not establish a formula for natural-sounding prose.

Google, Title links: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link

Titles should be descriptive, concise, and distinctive. Avoid keyword stuffing. Search title display can be truncated to fit available width; a character count is not a universal Google rule.

Google, Snippets: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet

Descriptions should accurately summarize the particular page. Search snippets can be generated from page content and vary by query; a supplied meta description is not guaranteed to appear.

## Recommendation and limitations

A single custom skill fits this request because original drafting, voice matching, fact preservation, and format-specific editing belong in one coherent workflow. Existing humanizer skills are useful candidates for rewriting alone. This recommendation is editorial judgment, not a measured ranking of all available tools.

Success should be judged by readers: usefulness, accuracy, clarity, voice fit, and whether the title's promise is fulfilled. No blind reader study or detector evaluation was performed for this custom skill. The GOV.UK writing guide was also sought, but its redirected page did not return usable body text; it was not used as evidence.

## Research verification, 10 November 2026

These studies support editorial choices, not a universal specification for human writing. Findings about particular models or English-language datasets should not be generalized to all languages or current models. Reported data were reviewed; the original experiments were not independently replicated.

### Reading effort

Oppenheimer (2006), Applied Cognitive Psychology: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.1178

The publisher's abstract reports five experiments; unnecessary vocabulary complexity reduced perceived author intelligence in the first three, with processing fluency mediating the effect. This supports familiar wording when meaning is preserved. It does not justify removing essential specialist terms or equating simplicity with truth. Access scope: publisher abstract; attempted full-text university PDF retrieval failed.

### Information that readers can find and use

Morkes and Nielsen (1997): https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/

Read the report's methods and results. Its measurement study involved 51 users and five versions of a sample website. The combined concise, scannable, objective condition scored 124% above the promotional control on a composite usability measure. This is a historical, task-specific result, not an expected gain for this skill or a rule for literary prose. Implication: retain purposeful headings and front-loaded answers; add explicit findability and comprehension review.

### Variation across genres

Zanotto and Aroyehun (EMNLP 2025): https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1163.pdf

Reviewed the paper's scope and reported findings: texts from eight domains and eleven models were compared on linguistic and stylistic features. Human texts tended toward simpler syntax and more diverse semantic content, with greater variability in the studied features. This supports reducing needless structural difficulty while retaining genre differences. It does not supply a sentence-length quota or prove any individual text's authorship.

### Personal voice is difficult to reproduce

Wang et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2025): https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.532.pdf

Reviewed the paper's task and reported results: over 40,000 generations per model, from more than 400 authors across news, email, forums, and blogs. Models approximated structured genres more successfully than nuanced informal styles. Implication: use relevant samples, compare observable choices, invite correction when useful, and avoid claiming perfect voice replication. The revised skill makes this limitation and feedback step explicit.

### More diverse vocabulary is not automatically more human

Kendro, Maloney, and Jarvis (2025): https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00086

Access scope: research abstract, not full paper. Four ChatGPT models were compared with 240 human writers across six lexical-diversity dimensions. The reported differences did not reduce to a rule that higher diversity equals more human writing. Implication: retain accurate terminology and meaningful repetition; reject lexical-diversity optimization as an editorial objective. This supports an explicit clarification in the revised skill, not a quantitative target.

### Detector scores are not quality measures

Liang et al. (2023), Patterns: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819v3

Read the study's results. Seven detectors were assessed on 91 TOEFL essays and 88 US eighth-grade essays; the reported average false-positive rate for the TOEFL set was 61.22%. These samples differ in context and population, and this is not a current performance estimate for every detector. It demonstrates why a score cannot establish writing quality or reliable authorship. The skill already rejects detector guarantees and invented authorship probabilities.

## Coverage verdict

The revised instructions align with the reviewed principles: appropriate vocabulary, manageable syntax, useful structure, genre sensitivity, cautious voice matching, factual fidelity, and reader-centered evaluation. This is evidence-informed design with editorial spot checks, not scientific certification or proof that all future outputs will sound human. Full-text data reanalysis and blinded reader testing remain unperformed.
