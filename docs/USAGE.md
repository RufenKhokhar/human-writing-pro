# Writing with Human Writing Pro

Give the assistant a subject, reader, purpose, and any facts it must preserve. Add voice samples when matching your style matters. A short task does not require a long brief.

## Article with title and description

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to write a 600-word beginner's guide to organizing a
small desk. Include one title and a short description. Keep the tone warm
and practical. Use ordinary household examples and avoid productivity claims.
```

## Rewrite without changing meaning

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to make this paragraph easier to read. Preserve every
number, condition, and uncertainty. Return the revised paragraph only:

In our 18-person pilot, participants reported saving about 20 minutes a week.
Results may differ across teams.
```

## Titles with a real limit

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to create five distinct titles for this article.
Each title must be at most 50 characters, including spaces. Give the count
beside each option and recommend one. Do not promise results absent from
the article: [paste article]
```

## Product description grounded in facts

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to write a 60-word product description.
Verified facts: a notebook with 160 numbered pages; it lies flat.
Audience: people who take handwritten work notes.
Do not invent paper weight, dimensions, warranties, or customer reviews.
```

## Match your voice

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to draft a newsletter introduction from the facts
below. These two samples are my writing for the same audience. Preserve
their level of formality, rhythm, and restrained humor without copying
their sentences. Use only the supplied facts for the new introduction.

Sample 1: [your text]
Sample 2: [your text]
New facts: [facts]
```

A close match may need feedback such as "shorter paragraphs", "less playful",
or "keep my regional phrasing". A sample is guidance, not a guarantee that
an assistant can reproduce every subtle feature of a person's writing.

## Research-based article

```text
Use Human Writing Pro to write an article answering [question] for [reader].
Research the current evidence using original sources. Distinguish findings
from inference. Include links near factual claims and explain meaningful
limitations. If you cannot browse, tell me which claims need verification.
```

## Give useful feedback

Point to the passage and describe the problem. "The opening takes too long
to reach the answer" is more actionable than "make it human". Supply facts
you want included instead of asking the assistant to invent lived experience.
